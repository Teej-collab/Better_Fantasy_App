# THE WEEKEND — Community Strategy

**Status:** Proposal for review.

---

## The goal: a recognizable participant, not an advertiser

The brief is explicit that this should not be spam, and that's the right instinct — fantasy football communities (Reddit, Discord especially) actively police and punish obvious self-promotion. The strategy here is to become a known, credible voice in existing communities before ever building THE WEEKEND's own, and to build THE WEEKEND's own community as a genuine gathering place, not a funnel.

## Where the community already lives

- **r/fantasyfootball** (and format-specific subs like r/DynastyFF, r/Fantasy_Football) — the largest concentration of exactly the audience in `02_Target_Audience.md`. Real, high-intent threads about platform frustration happen here constantly.
- **League-specific and general fantasy Discord servers** — smaller, higher-trust environments than Reddit, often centered around a specific podcast, creator, or league group.
- **Fantasy football podcasts' own communities** (Discord servers, Patreon communities, comment sections) — see `10_Creator_Strategy.md` for the outreach angle; the community angle is about *participating*, not pitching.
- **Facebook groups** for local/regional or work leagues — real but harder to measure and lower-priority given the team's size; monitor rather than actively invest early.

## How THE WEEKEND participates without becoming an advertiser

1. **Answer real questions honestly**, including when the honest answer isn't flattering to THE WEEKEND ("no, we don't have auction drafts yet" is more trust-building long-term than dodging the question). This directly extends the brand-voice rule in `01_Brand_Positioning.md`.
2. **Be a fantasy football participant first, a builder second.** Engage with general fantasy discussion (waiver-wire debates, trade advice) the way any knowledgeable fantasy player would, with no product mention — most of this engagement should never reference THE WEEKEND at all.
3. **Identify and support existing small league-specific and format-specific communities** rather than trying to pull people out of them into a THE WEEKEND-branded space prematurely — the product should meet people where they already are for as long as possible.

## THE WEEKEND's own community (Discord)

- **When to launch:** Phase 3 (March-April 2027, per `04_12_Month_Strategy.md`) — not before there's a real product and a real (even small) user base to seed it with; an empty Discord server is worse than no Discord server.
- **Purpose:** A home for Founding Leagues and early commissioners to talk to each other and to the founder directly — this is a genuine support/community channel, and its existence is itself a trust signal ("there's a real place to go if something breaks").
- **Structure:** Kept small and simple — a general channel, a bugs/feedback channel (real, visible responsiveness here is a marketing asset in itself, consistent with the "building in public" pillar), a league-showcase channel where commissioners can post their own league's content.
- **Growth:** Seeded directly from the Founding Leagues cohort (`15_2027_Launch_Campaign.md`) and from genuine Reddit/podcast-community participants who've already engaged positively elsewhere — not from a broad "join our Discord" push.

## Creator and podcast communities — see `10_Creator_Strategy.md`

The overlap between "community strategy" and "creator strategy" is real; this document covers organic participation in *audiences* creators and podcasts have already built, while `10_Creator_Strategy.md` covers direct outreach and partnership with the creators themselves.

## What "genuine participation" rules out

- Sockpuppet accounts, seeded "organic-looking" posts, or paying for engagement — beyond being against most of these platforms' rules, this is exactly the kind of thing that would be discovered and would permanently damage trust in a community this size and this interconnected.
- Posting the same promotional content across many subreddits/servers simultaneously — each community should get participation tailored to what's actually being discussed there, not a copy-pasted campaign.
- Treating community management as a broadcast job — the actual job is reading, replying, and being useful, which does not scale the way content production does and should be budgeted as real founder/team time, not automated.
